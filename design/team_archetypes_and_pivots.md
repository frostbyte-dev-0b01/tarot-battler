# Team Archetypes and Pivot Units

## Core Principle

Team archetypes should be defined by tactical and strategic behavior, not by tarot-flavored labels.

Tarot flavor should live in:
- character identities
- ability and passive names
- status vocabulary
- presentation and theme

The archetypes themselves should answer practical questions:
- how does this team try to win?
- what pressure does it create?
- what does it force the opponent to answer?

## Recommended Tactical Archetypes

### 1. Front-to-Back Pressure

Goal:
- break the frontline efficiently and win by stable formation advantage

Typical tools:
- direct frontline damage
- anti-tank effects
- row-locked support
- durable frontliners

Strategic value:
- clear baseline archetype
- rewards disciplined positioning
- vulnerable to backline reach, disruption, or superior sustain

### 2. Backline Reach

Goal:
- pressure supports, mages, and fragile utility pieces behind the frontline

Typical tools:
- backline targeting
- same-column attacks
- splash centered around protected targets
- marks for focus fire

Strategic value:
- makes backline placement meaningful
- punishes greedy support structures

### 3. Status Pressure

Goal:
- accumulate stack-based disadvantages that demand answers

Typical tools:
- stack application
- status amplification
- payoff effects
- spread, transfer, or detonation

Strategic value:
- creates strong build-around teams
- becomes much deeper when cleanse, dispel, and payoff tools exist

### 4. Sustain / Stabilization

Goal:
- survive early pressure and win long fights

Typical tools:
- targeted healing
- all-team sustain
- cleanse
- defensive buffs
- SPI recovery

Strategic value:
- anchors longer games
- should beat fair aggression but lose to anti-heal, backline reach, or burst

### 5. Tempo / Burst

Goal:
- create short windows of overwhelming pressure before the opponent stabilizes

Typical tools:
- opening buffs
- SPI acceleration
- kill chaining
- focused burst
- snowball triggers

Strategic value:
- makes sequencing and rule priority matter
- should be explosive but not durable

### 6. Disruption / Control

Goal:
- interfere with enemy execution instead of simply overpowering them

Typical tools:
- dispels
- taunt or intercept
- target redirection
- stuns or turn denial
- anti-trigger or anti-buff tools

Strategic value:
- creates counterplay depth
- should punish linear strategies without becoming oppressive

### Optional Expansion Archetypes

These are also valuable once the base ecosystem is strong:

- **Resource Advantage**
  SPI generation, SPI denial, SPI transfer, cost manipulation

- **Death / Sacrifice**
  on-death value, martyr setups, low-HP incentives, kill sequencing traps

## Archetype Ecosystem Goal

These archetypes should not form a rigid rock-paper-scissors chart, but they should create enough counter-pressure that matchup prediction matters.

Healthy examples:
- Front-to-Back Pressure threatens fragile setup
- Backline Reach punishes sustain greed
- Sustain outlasts fair aggression
- Tempo punishes slow setup
- Disruption punishes linear engines
- Status Pressure punishes teams without answers

## Multi-Role Pivot Units

One of the best ways to deepen team building is to include units that can fill multiple roles depending on their rules and loadout.

These units should not be generically strong at everything. Their value comes from flexibility.

### Why Pivot Units Matter

Pivot units create strategic depth because they connect:
- loadout decisions
- rule scripting
- matchup prediction
- hidden flexibility in team preview

A good pivot unit makes the player decide:
- which role to bias toward
- how to distribute stats
- how to script the pivot
- what matchups it is supposed to answer

### Good Pattern

Multi-role units should trade peak efficiency for flexibility.

That means:
- a specialist is stronger in its lane
- a pivot unit is more adaptable
- the pivot unit only outperforms specialists when the player scripts and positions it well

### Example: Death

Death is a strong candidate for a pivot design.

Possible shape:
- balanced STR and INT
- physical and magical actives both equipped
- rules that switch between them based on defender profile
- execute-style condition when target HP falls below a threshold

That makes Death:
- flexible
- matchup-sensitive
- skill-testing through rule design

It also creates a meaningful drafting decision:
- do I want a pure specialist or a flexible answer piece?

### Recommended Pivot Roles

Useful pivot patterns include:

- offense / offense
  physical vs magical attacker depending on target defenses

- offense / support
  attacks in some matchups, stabilizes or buffs in others

- sustain / disruption
  healer that can also cleanse or dispel based on board state

- frontline / payoff
  durable unit that becomes threatening once conditions are met

### Good Candidates for Pivot Design

Cards that naturally fit this pattern:
- Death
- Temperance
- Magician
- Lovers
- Justice
- Wheel of Fortune
- Devil
- Judgement

### Design Rules for Pivot Units

- Cover 2 roles, not too many
- Make the pivot scriptable through rules
- Make the pivot visible in hindsight from battle logs
- Do not let the unit be best-in-class at both roles simultaneously
- Tie the pivot to matchup, formation, or resource state

## Practical Recommendation

When designing future cards, ask two questions:

1. Which tactical archetype does this card support or counter?
2. Is this card a specialist, enabler, counter-tech piece, or pivot unit?

That keeps the roster strategically coherent while still allowing strong tarot flavor.

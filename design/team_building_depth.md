# Team Building Depth Notes

## Core Principle

If deep team building is the core loop, the most valuable mechanics are not raw damage or stat buffs. The strongest additions are effects that change how a whole team behaves, what it cares about, and how it interacts with opposing teams.

The goal is to create compositions, counters, and tradeoffs rather than just stronger standalone units.

## High-Value Design Axes

### 1. Positional Economies

Formation should be more than row protection.

Good examples:
- units that protect the ally behind them
- units that buff companions or same-column allies
- units that gain bonuses when isolated
- attacks or passives that care about columns, adjacency, or lanes

This makes formation itself part of team building.

### 2. Resource Engines

SPI should be a team-building axis, not just a cost gate.

Good examples:
- SPI generation for companions or allies
- SPI transfer between allies
- SPI denial or drain on enemies
- alternative costs such as spending HP instead of SPI

This creates sequencing and engine-building decisions.

### 3. Status Ecosystems

Statuses become interesting when teams can do more than simply apply them.

Good examples:
- consume Poison or Bleed for burst damage
- convert enemy debuffs into ally buffs
- amplify, spread, cleanse, or steal statuses
- trigger bonuses when statuses are applied or removed

This turns status play into a full archetype.

### 4. Targeting and Routing Control

Interesting teams should be able to influence who gets hit and when.

Good examples:
- taunt
- intercept
- row bypass
- marks that make enemies easier to focus
- effects that redirect attacks or punish targeting choices

This makes team building about combat routing, not only numbers.

### 5. Trigger Webs

Passives and abilities get deeper when they care about specific battle events.

Useful triggers:
- first ally damaged
- ally death
- enemy healed
- companion acts
- same target hit multiple times
- status removed

These effects create synergy webs rather than isolated power.

### 6. Role Compression and Tradeoffs

Cards are more interesting when they can solve multiple jobs if built around properly.

Good examples:
- a healer that becomes a cleanser or anti-debuff specialist
- a tank that converts overheal into reflect
- a mage that can play burst or control depending on allies

This gives players meaningful composition decisions.

### 7. Counter-Tech

A deep team-builder needs tools that answer dominant strategies.

Important tech spaces:
- anti-buff
- anti-heal
- anti-status
- anti-backline pressure
- anti-death-trigger
- anti-resource

This keeps prediction and metagame reading central to the game.

## Design Heuristic

When adding an ability or passive, ask:

- Does this create a new archetype?
- Does this counter an existing archetype?
- Does this make formation matter more?
- Does this make rules matter more?
- Does this create a meaningful drafting or loadout tradeoff?

If the main effect is only “more damage” or “more stats,” it is usually low-value for long-term strategic depth.

## Recommended Priority Areas

The next mechanics that most improve team-building depth are:

1. Companion and formation synergies
2. Status payoff and transformation effects
3. Targeting manipulation
4. SPI economy mechanics
5. Counter-tech tools

## Practical Direction

The strongest version of the game is likely one where:

- each tarot card supports one or two clear archetypes
- archetypes overlap partially rather than living in isolation
- compositions have real counters and pivots
- formation and rule scripting meaningfully change outcomes

That creates a game where weekly rebuilding is driven by strategic structure, not only numerical efficiency.
